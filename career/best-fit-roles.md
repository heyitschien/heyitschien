Your best job entry

The Tao here is: don’t chase the job directly all day. Enter the stream where useful technology work is already happening, solve small real problems repeatedly, and let trust turn into opportunity.
If I were you, I would make one six-month bet:
Become known in Los Angeles as an AI operations and product-operations builder who turns messy human needs into requirements, AI-assisted execution, validation, documentation, and adoption.
That is already what you naturally do. We need to make other people see it and experience it.

Your six-month operating loop
Month 1: Enter the rooms
Apply to no more than two excellent roles each week.
Join Hack for LA, AI LA and PMA.LA.
Register for AI Tinkerers.
Create one simple portfolio landing page with three proof pieces:
AI-agent coordination system
Cindy paid implementation
Autonomous trading-system validation workflow
Prepare one three-minute demonstration.
Months 2–3: Become useful
Choose one real team project and contribute every week.
Take small, visible ownership:
map a confusing workflow;
write requirements and acceptance criteria;
test a feature and document failures;
improve onboarding instructions;
build a small AI-assisted automation;
organize project knowledge so others can resume work.
Do not offer unlimited free development. Offer bounded value: one workflow audit, one prototype, one test plan or one documentation improvement.
Months 3–4: Build external trust
By this point, aim to have:
one shipped team contribution;
one project lead who can recommend you;
one public case study;
one live demo;
five to ten people who understand what you can do.
This transforms your résumé from “self-directed learner” into “person trusted by a real team.”
Months 5–6: Convert trust into paid work
Ask project collaborators for introductions.
Apply through warm contacts whenever possible.
Pursue small paid AI-workflow implementations for local businesses and nonprofits.
Target one or two strong applications weekly.
Use each interview rejection to identify one precise gap—not to restart your whole career plan.
Weekly scoreboard
Every week:
2 excellent applications
1 real project contribution
2 meaningful professional follow-ups
1 visible artifact or build update
1 local event every two weeks
3–5 hours improving Excel/Sheets, basic SQL, APIs/JSON or product/UAT practices
Six months of that produces approximately 40–50 tailored applications, 20+ real-team contributions, several public artifacts and a genuine local network.
How to introduce yourself
Use this:
I’m an AI operations and product-operations builder. I turn messy workflows into clear requirements, use AI agents to help build and test solutions, and document the system so people can actually adopt it. I’m currently looking to contribute to a real Los Angeles team and own a small workflow from problem through validated result.
Then ask:
What repetitive, confusing or poorly documented process is slowing your team down right now?
That question is your Tao. You are not begging someone to give you a job. You are looking for a real point of friction where your natural ability creates value.
Six months is enough to materially change your candidacy. The missing ingredient is not more interest, another generic certificate or hundreds of applications. It is outside evidence that another team trusted you to use AI and computer systems to produce a real result.
